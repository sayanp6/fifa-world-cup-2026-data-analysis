# 2026 FIFA World Cup Data Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyses the 2026 FIFA World Cup using Microsoft Excel, combining team and player data to investigate tournament performance and progression.

The project compares pre-tournament team rankings with actual group-stage finishes and uses these rankings to create a hypothetical knockout-stage pathway. Player data is also analysed using performance rankings, descriptive statistics, correlation and linear regression.

## Key Areas of Analysis

### Team Analysis
- Comparison of expected and actual group-stage finishes
- Identification of teams that exceeded or fell below their expected positions
- Hypothetical knockout-stage pathway based on pre-tournament rankings
- Comparison of expected and actual knockout progression

### Player Analysis
- Top scorers
- Most efficient scorers using goals per appearance
- Most used players
- Goal contribution leaders using goals and assists
- Discipline rankings using yellow and red cards

### Statistical Analysis
- Descriptive statistics including mean, median, minimum, maximum and standard deviation
- Correlation analysis between appearances, goals and assists
- Analysis of goals per appearance
- Linear regression examining the relationship between appearances and goals
- Regression fit, R², coefficient and statistical significance

## Key Findings

- 25 of 48 teams (52.1%) finished in their expected group position.
- 11 teams finished above their expected position, while 12 finished below it.
- Goals and appearances had a positive correlation of approximately 0.36.
- Goals and assists also showed a positive correlation of approximately 0.35.
- The regression model found a positive relationship between appearances and goals, with an R² of approximately 0.127.
- The relatively low R² demonstrates that appearances alone explain only a limited proportion of differences in player goal output.

## Limitations and Future Development

The analysis is based on a defined set of team and player variables. Player appearances do not account for minutes played, while factors such as position, tactical role, shots and chances created are not included.

Future development could include additional player and team performance variables, multiple regression models and comparisons with previous FIFA World Cup tournaments. These additions could provide a more detailed understanding of the factors associated with team and player performance.
